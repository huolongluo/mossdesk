# How we run MossDesk — written narrative

MossDesk started from a boring, expensive fact: the front office of a small business is still a person with a phone. Quotes live in WhatsApp. Invoices live in a notebook. Follow-up happens at midnight, or not at all. The shops that need an operator most cannot hire one, and they will not sit through a six-week “AI transformation.” They will pay for someone who takes the mess, decides, and hands back something they can run tomorrow morning.

We built that someone as a firm, not a feature. The customer does not chat with a model until a useful paragraph appears. They open a job. Five Gemini agents execute in sequence, and each one is allowed to bind the business.

Scout writes the operating picture: who the customer is, the actual pain, constraints, what success looks like, and the risk flags. No later agent is allowed to skip this. Pricer is the commercial officer. It takes or declines the job, picks Desk Sprint or retainer, and sets a USD price inside a published policy: floor $29, ceiling $249, complexity bands, and an automatic decline if the request is legal or medical advice. That price is the invoice. A founder is not allowed to “just make it $9 so we get a logo.” Operator is the delivery team. It produces the product we sell — copy-paste scripts, a seven-day sequence, metrics, risks. Collector decides terms and the dunning cadence. Auditor is the partner who can still kill the job. Quality under 70 cannot ship. Escalations are first-class status, not a toast message.

This split is how we use AI day to day. Gemini is not a writing assistant sitting beside a human playbook. Gemini is the playbook. The human role is narrow on purpose: keep the Stripe account honest, handle the jobs Auditor refuses, talk to the first shops, and not lie to judges about revenue. If a job is escalated, a person reads the log and either restates the problem or refunds. We do not silently edit the Pricer’s number.

The economic story beyond the founding team is the point of the Small Business Services category. Every package is meant to be executed by the shop’s existing people — the owner, the one front-desk hire, the apprentice who already answers the phone. We are not replacing that person with a bot that talks to their customers unsupervised. We are selling the missing manager: rules for when to stop cooking for non-payers, a deposit script before the plumber leaves the house, a no-show cadence a gym can run without hiring a second receptionist. If MossDesk works, those businesses keep more of the work they already won. That is cash in the neighborhood, not a SaaS dashboard that looks busy.

Building this way changed the product. The first instinct in a hackathon is a magic text box. The tape on `/ops` forced a different shape. If an agent cannot name its decision in a verb — SET_PRICE, PACKAGE_READY, INVOICE_TERMS, SHIP, ESCALATE — it does not belong in the firm. Latency, model id, and rationale are stored with the job because judges, and later customers, should be able to ask “why did you charge $79?” and get an answer that is not a vibe.

We host on Cloud Run so the same binary judges click is the one that ran the agents, and we call Gemini on every job rather than replaying fixtures. Stripe is the only path we will report as revenue. The local demo-payment button exists so a reviewer without cards can still walk the loop; it is labeled and excluded from the P&L. Related-party charges, if any, will be listed separately as the rules require.

What we still have to prove in the remaining hours is the part no repository can fake: an arms-length customer who pays. The model is a priced sprint that can become a retainer when the mess is clearly ongoing. The cost structure is Gemini tokens, Cloud Run, and Stripe fees — small enough that a handful of sprints cover the month. Sustainability is not a slide. It is whether a bakery will pay again when the next catering season starts. That is the test we are taking into production, with the agents already on the clock.
